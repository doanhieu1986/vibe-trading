# Vietnam Macroeconomics for Equity Analysis

## Overview

Vietnamese equity returns are dominated by three macro engines: SBV credit policy (banking + real estate), USD/VND stability (FDI confidence + foreign flows), and export-driven GDP (electronics, textiles, footwear). Unlike developed markets, monetary transmission runs primarily through credit growth quotas — not interest rates alone — and fiscal disbursement of public investment is a discretely-timed equity catalyst (steel, construction, IZ developers). This skill maps the macro framework an equity analyst must hold before running sector or single-stock work.

Applicable scenarios:
- Translating SBV rate / credit room announcements into bank NIM and real estate liquidity views
- Reading USD/VND moves as flow / risk signals
- Anticipating quarterly fiscal disbursement and its impact on cyclical sectors
- Sizing macro tail risks: rate depreciation, US tariffs, real estate bond rollovers
- Aligning portfolio tilt with Vietnam's seasonal earnings cycle

## Core Concepts

### State Bank of Vietnam (SBV) Monetary Policy

SBV uses multiple policy rates and quantitative tools simultaneously:

| Tool | Vietnamese name | What it does |
|------|-----------------|--------------|
| Refinancing rate | Lãi suất tái cấp vốn | Headline policy rate; SBV lending to banks |
| Discount rate | Lãi suất tái chiết khấu | Lower floor; SBV discount window |
| OMO (open market) | Nghiệp vụ thị trường mở | Daily liquidity injection via reverse repo of G-bonds |
| Required reserve ratio | Tỷ lệ dự trữ bắt buộc | Reserves on deposits; rarely changed |
| Credit growth quota | Hạn mức tăng trưởng tín dụng | **Per-bank annual cap** on loan book growth |
| Deposit rate ceiling | Trần lãi suất tiền gửi | Cap on short-term retail deposit rates (admin tool) |

```
2023–2024 cycle:
  - SBV cut refinancing rate 4 times (Mar, Apr, May, Jun 2023): 6.0% -> 4.5%
  - Credit growth target each year: ~14–15% (binding quota allocated by bank)
  - Stock impact: banks (mixed NIM compression vs volume) + real estate (rate relief)

2024–2025 cycle:
  - Held rates flat; renewed quotas; deposit rate ceiling lifted gradually
```

Credit growth quota is the most uniquely Vietnamese tool: each year SBV announces a system-wide target (e.g., 14%) and allocates it bank-by-bank based on health metrics. Banks that hit their quota mid-year must stop new lending until SBV raises the cap — this directly throttles real-estate developer financing.

### Exchange Rate: USD/VND

```
Regime:        Managed float with central reference rate ("tỷ giá trung tâm") set daily by SBV
Trading band:  ±5% around the daily reference rate
Typical level: USD/VND 23,500 (2021) -> 24,500 (2023) -> 25,500 (2024) -> 26,000+ (2025)
Drivers:
  - DXY (US dollar index)
  - China CNY (high correlation, ~0.7+)
  - Trade balance (Vietnam runs a goods surplus, services deficit)
  - FDI inflow (chronic positive)
  - Capital outflow (carry unwind risk, repatriation)
  - SBV FX reserves and intervention
SBV reaction function:
  - When VND weakens past upper band -> SBV sells USD reserves to defend
  - 2022 episode: SBV burned ~USD 25bn of reserves; equity market fell concurrently
```

Equity transmission:
- Importers (steel inputs, fuel, pharma raw materials) suffer from VND depreciation
- Exporters (textiles, seafood, electronics OEM) benefit, though many are unlisted
- Banks: indirect FX exposure via dollar deposits and corporate FX loans
- Foreign investors: VND weakness directly erodes USD-translated returns -> outflow loop

### Inflation: CPI

```
CPI target:     ~4% YoY (set by National Assembly annually)
Composition (weights, ~2024):
  - Food and food services: ~33%
  - Housing, electricity, water, fuel: ~18%
  - Transportation: ~10%
  - Education, healthcare: ~12% (administered)
  - Other: ~27%

Key drivers:
  - Pork prices (single largest food line, ~3.5% of CPI basket alone)
  - Electricity tariff hikes (EVN price increases are political events)
  - Fuel prices (managed by MOIT every 10 days)
  - Tet seasonal food surge (Q1 print elevated)

Print cadence: GSO publishes ~25th–28th of each month, CPI MoM + YoY for current month.
```

### GDP: Growth Engine

```
Growth target:  6–7% YoY (set by National Assembly)
Realized:       7%+ in 2018, ~2.6% in 2021 (COVID), ~8% 2022, ~5% 2023, ~6.5% 2024

Drivers (export-led):
  1. FDI manufacturing exports: electronics (Samsung ~20% of total exports!), Intel, LG, Foxconn
  2. Textiles, footwear, furniture exports to US / EU
  3. Domestic consumption (rising middle class)
  4. Public investment / infrastructure
  5. Tourism (recovering post-COVID)

Print cadence:
  - Quarterly GDP: GSO releases ~28th–29th of quarter-end month (Mar, Jun, Sep, Dec)
  - Industrial production (IIP): monthly ~28th
  - Retail sales: monthly ~28th
  - PMI (S&P Global Vietnam Manufacturing PMI): 1st of next month
```

### Fiscal Policy and Public Investment Disbursement

Public investment ("đầu tư công") is a key cyclical lever:

```
Budget cycle:
  - National Assembly approves annual plan (~Nov of prior year)
  - Q1: typically slow disbursement (Tet, plan rollover)
  - Q2–Q3: ramp
  - Q4: government pushes hard to hit annual target -> end-of-year sprint

Disbursement rate = actual / planned for year (target ~95%, often misses at 80–90%)

Sector beneficiaries when disbursement accelerates:
  - Construction contractors: VCG, HHV, C4G, CTD
  - Steel: HPG, HSG (long products for rebar)
  - Cement: HT1, BCC
  - Stone / asphalt: KSB, DHA
  - Industrial zone developers (via FDI-linked infrastructure): KBC, SZC, IDC

Mega-projects to track:
  - Long Thanh International Airport (2021–2026 buildout)
  - North-South Expressway (multi-phase)
  - Ring Road 4 Hanoi, Ring Road 3 Ho Chi Minh City
  - Hanoi & HCMC metro lines
```

### Real Estate Credit and Bond Regulations

The 2022–2023 real estate crisis revealed the developer–bond–bank loop. Two key regulatory texts:

```
Circular 16/2021/TT-NHNN (revised by Circular 03/2023):
  - Restricts banks' purchase of corporate bonds
  - Tightens conditions for banks rolling over developer debt
  - Implication: cuts off shadow funding channel for distressed developers

Decree 65/2022/ND-CP (revised by Decree 08/2023):
  - Tightens private corporate bond issuance to professional investors only
  - Mandates credit ratings, disclosure
  - Decree 08 (Mar 2023): allowed temporary debt restructuring and bond-for-asset swap
  - Implication: primary bond market essentially frozen 2022–2023

NVL (Novaland), PDR (Phat Dat), HPX (Hai Phat) crisis 2022–2023:
  - Bond maturities triggered margin calls and forced sell of pledged shares
  - 5+ consecutive floor closes; VHM held up by Vingroup credit
  - Recovery began Q2 2024 as Decree 08 restructuring took hold
```

### Sovereign Credit Rating

```
S&P:      BB+ (positive outlook, upgraded 2022) — one notch below investment grade
Moody's:  Ba2 (stable, upgraded 2022)
Fitch:    BB+ (stable, upgraded 2023)

Implication: dollar bond spreads moderate; sovereign-corporate linkage limited
Targeted upgrade to investment grade (BBB-) is a key government objective by 2030.
Watch items: fiscal deficit, public debt / GDP (~37%), external reserves, banking NPLs
```

### Key Risk Factors for VN Equity Macro

1. **USD/VND depreciation pressure**: weak CNY + strong DXY -> SBV must choose between reserves burn and rate hike
2. **US tariff risk**: Trump-era 25% China tariffs were Vietnam's tailwind; new Section 301 risk against Vietnam itself or anti-circumvention duties is a tail risk
3. **Real estate debt overhang**: ~USD 30–40bn in corporate bonds outstanding, weighted to developers; rollover gap persists
4. **China slowdown**: ~10% of exports go to China; commodity demand affects steel; tourism affects retail / aviation
5. **Climate**: drought (Mekong / hydro generation), typhoon (industrial north) — direct GDP hits
6. **Regulatory shocks**: anti-corruption campaign ("đốt lò") creates executive uncertainty; periodic crackdowns on financial sector

### Sector Sensitivity to Macro Variables

| Sector | Primary macro driver | Direction | Notes |
|--------|----------------------|-----------|-------|
| Banking | Credit growth quota + NIM | + quota, ambiguous NIM | NIM falls when SBV cuts rates faster than deposit lag |
| Real estate | Credit availability + bond market | + loose credit | Pháp lý (legal clearance) is a structural overlay |
| Steel | Public investment + China steel price | + capex, − China oversupply | HPG sensitive to coking coal cost |
| Retail | Consumer confidence + real wage | + wage growth | MWG / FRT sensitive to discretionary spend |
| F&B | Inflation (input vs pricing) | Mixed | VNM defensive; SAB beer premium-sensitive |
| Tech (FPT) | FDI tech ecosystem + USD revenue | + USD strength | FPT software exports priced in USD |
| Energy | Oil prices + regulated pricing | Mixed | GAS = upstream gas; PLX = retail fuel margin (regulated) |
| Aviation | Fuel + tourism | − fuel, + tourism | VJC vs HVN balance-sheet difference matters |
| Industrial zones | FDI flow + US tariffs on China | + China+1 | KBC, SZC long-term beneficiaries |

### Key Macro Release Calendar

```
Monthly:
  - GSO: CPI MoM/YoY (~25th–28th)
  - GSO: Industrial Production Index (IIP) (~28th)
  - GSO: Retail sales, trade balance (~28th)
  - SBV: Monthly credit growth print (~early next month)
  - S&P Global / IHS Markit: Vietnam Manufacturing PMI (1st of next month)
  - MOIT: Fuel price adjustment (every ~10 days)

Quarterly:
  - GSO: GDP print (~28th–29th of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec)
  - Listed company earnings: filing deadline T+30 days (preliminary) / T+45 days (audited)

Annually:
  - Oct–Dec: National Assembly approves next-year GDP / CPI / credit growth targets
  - Jan: SBV announces per-bank credit growth quota allocation
```

### Seasonal Patterns

```
Q1 (Jan–Mar):
  - Tet holiday closes market for ~1 week (late Jan / early Feb)
  - Pre-Tet retail spend (positive for MWG, FRT)
  - Slow industrial activity, weak public investment disbursement
  - Q1 GDP print typically lowest of year

Q2 (Apr–Jun):
  - Earnings season for FY-prior + Q1 fresh
  - April 30 / May 1 holidays
  - Annual general meetings dominate news flow
  - Public investment ramp begins

Q3 (Jul–Sep):
  - Q2 earnings release (Jul–Aug)
  - Mid-year credit quota review by SBV (top-up announcements)
  - VN30 semiannual rebalance (effective ~early Aug and early Feb)
  - Sep 2–3 National Day holiday

Q4 (Oct–Dec):
  - Q3 earnings release (Oct–Nov)
  - Public investment sprint (steel / construction tailwind)
  - National Assembly session sets next-year targets (late Oct–Nov)
  - Year-end window-dressing flows
```

## Analysis Framework

```

  SBV refinancing rate: X.XX%  (last move: cut/hike on YYYY-MM-DD)
  Credit growth YTD:    XX.X% / annual quota XX%
  OMO net position:     net +/- VND XXt this week

Inflation / Growth:
  CPI YoY:              X.X% (target 4%)
  GDP latest qtr:       X.X% YoY
  PMI:                  XX.X (above/below 50)

FX / External:
  USD/VND:              XX,XXX  (band: ±5% of XX,XXX ref)
  FX reserves est:      USD XXX bn  (months of imports: X.X)
  Trade balance YTD:    +USD XXX bn

Real estate / Credit risk:
  Property bonds 12M maturity:  ~VND XXX tr
  Major developer headlines:     [list]

Equity flow proxies:
  Foreign net buy/sell MTD:      VND XXX bn
  Margin balance:                VND XXX tr (YoY growth XX%)
```

### 2. Sector Tilt Heuristics

```
SBV easing (rate cut, credit quota loosening):
  Overweight: banks (asset growth), real estate, securities, construction
  Underweight: defensive consumer staples (relative)

SBV tightening:
  Overweight: cash, F&B (VNM), utilities
  Underweight: real estate (high beta), securities, marginal developers

VND depreciation > 3% in 30 days:
  Overweight: exporters (textiles, tech services -- FPT), IZ developers
  Underweight: importers (steel input cost, pharma), aviation (fuel + USD lease cost)

Public investment acceleration (disbursement % > 70% by Q3):
  Overweight: HPG, HSG, VCG, HHV, KSB, cement (HT1)

China slowdown / commodity weakness:
  Overweight: domestic consumer plays
  Underweight: steel (China dump risk), industrial zones if FDI delays

US tariff shock on Vietnam:
  Overweight: domestic-focused (banks, real estate, retail)
  Underweight: FDI-export-linked (IZ, textile, electronics OEM)
```

### 3. Macro Risk Scorecard

```
For a given portfolio, score each macro risk 1 (low) to 5 (high) and weight by exposure:

Rate risk:        Σ(banking weight × NIM sensitivity)  +  Σ(real estate weight × funding sensitivity)
FX risk:          Σ(import-heavy sector weight × 1)  −  Σ(export-heavy weight × 1)
Tariff risk:      Σ(FDI-linked sector weight)
Credit cycle:     Σ(real estate + securities + leveraged corporate weight)
Inflation:        Σ(consumer / regulated price exposure)

Overall macro VaR = portfolio beta × VN-Index 1-day 99% VaR (~2.5%) × position size
```

## Notes

1. **Data quality**: GSO data has been revised in past; PMI and HSBC PMI are more timely than official IIP
2. **Communication style**: SBV does not pre-commit rate paths like Fed / ECB; surprise moves are common, watch SBV Governor press statements
3. **Off-balance-sheet bank exposure**: corporate bonds held by banks plus interbank may understate real-estate exposure; check Circular 16 disclosures
4. **Anti-corruption cycle effect**: high-profile arrests can briefly freeze decision-making in regulated sectors (banking M&A, real estate approvals)
5. **Don't ignore Tet base effects**: Q1 YoY comparisons are noisy because Tet shifts within Jan/Feb; use 2-month combined (Jan+Feb) prints for cleaner signal
6. **Cross-check with Vietstock / Cafef daily flow data**: foreign net buy and margin balance are the two real-time macro flow proxies most useful for short-horizon decisions
