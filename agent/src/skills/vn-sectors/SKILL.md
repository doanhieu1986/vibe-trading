# Vietnam Sector Landscape

## Overview

The Vietnamese equity market is concentrated: banking alone is ~35% of VN-Index weight, and the top 5 sectors cover ~80%. Each sector has its own drivers, key tickers, and policy sensitivities. This skill is a practitioner's map of the eight sectors that matter most for equity research and a guide to sector rotation patterns. Combine with `vn-equity-market` (microstructure), `vn-macro` (drivers), and `vn-derivatives` (hedging tools) for a complete VN equity toolkit.

Applicable scenarios:
- Building VN equity universes by sector for screening
- Sector rotation positioning around macro / policy events
- Single-stock research targeting (identify the relevant 2–3 peers per sector)
- Risk concentration analysis (sector caps and beta)
- Mapping policy / regulation changes to sector winners and losers

### Banking — Largest Sector

```
Composition:
  SOE banks (state-controlled, MOF/SBV majority):
    - VCB (Vietcombank) — best asset quality, premium valuation
    - BID (BIDV) — largest by assets
    - CTG (VietinBank) — third SOE pillar

  Private banks:
    - VPB (VPBank) — consumer finance via FE Credit (post-divestment)
    - TCB (Techcombank) — corporate + real-estate exposure, Masan/Vingroup ecosystem
    - MBB (Military Bank) — military-affiliated, strong CASA ratio
    - ACB (Asia Commercial Bank) — retail focus, conservative
    - STB (Sacombank) — restructuring story
    - HDB (HDBank) — mid-size
    - SHB, MSB, OCB, LPB, TPB, SSB, EIB ...

Key metrics:
  NIM:           net interest margin (3.0–4.5% typical for VN banks)
  CASA:          current account / total deposits (high CASA = cheap funding; TCB and MBB lead at 35–45%)
  NPL ratio:     non-performing loans / total loans (reported 1.5–3%, but Group 2 / restructured loans matter)
  LDR:           loan-to-deposit ratio (capped 85% by SBV)
  CAR (Basel II):  capital adequacy ratio (>8% minimum, banks running ~9–12%)
  Credit growth: vs SBV quota
  ROE:           return on equity (15–25% top tier)
  PB ratio:      valuation; VCB premium 2.5–3x PB, sector average 1.0–1.5x PB

Macro sensitivity:
  Rate cuts:       NIM compression near-term but loan demand recovery medium-term
  Credit quota:    quota top-up = direct positive for loan book growth
  NPL formation:   real-estate stress flows to bank balance sheet via developer exposure
  VAMC:            "bad bank" holding legacy NPLs; many banks still amortizing VAMC bonds
  Basel III roadmap: forthcoming, will tighten capital

Investment angles:
  Cyclical recovery: BID, CTG (state catch-up)
  Quality compounders: VCB, MBB, ACB
  Real estate exposure: TCB (Vingroup link), VPB (consumer finance)
  Distressed restructuring: STB, EIB (special situations)
```

### Real Estate — High Beta, High Drama

```
Subsegments:
  Residential developers:
    - VHM (Vinhomes) — flagship Vingroup affordable + premium developer, ~70% of profit
    - NVL (Novaland) — second largest, distressed since 2022 bond crisis
    - KDH (Khang Dien) — Ho Chi Minh City focus, conservative balance sheet
    - DXG (Dat Xanh) — diversified developer + brokerage
    - PDR (Phat Dat) — distressed during 2022
    - NLG (Nam Long) — Japanese JV partners, suburban
    - HDG (Ha Do) — diversified energy + property
    - DIG, CEO — Hanoi-focused, smaller

  Retail real estate / mall:
    - VRE (Vincom Retail) — mall operator, Vingroup

  Industrial / Resort:
    - VIC (Vingroup parent holding) — VinFast EV exposure
    - BCM (Becamex) — Binh Duong industrial / urban
    - DPG (Dat Phuong) — niche

Key concepts:
  Pháp lý (legal clearance):
    Vietnam land law requires sequential approvals (1/500 master plan -> investment registration ->
    land use right certificate -> construction permit -> sale license).
    Projects often stuck mid-pipeline for years. "Pháp lý sạch" (clean legal) is the #1 risk filter.

  Pre-sale (bán hàng):
    Developers recognize revenue only on hand-over (~2–3 yrs after pre-sale).
    Cash inflow is from deposits and progress payments during construction.
    Watch "pre-sale value" (giá trị bán hàng) as forward-revenue indicator.

  Bond rollover wall:
    2022 issuance peak -> 2024–2025 maturities bunched -> rollover stress
    Decree 65 + 08 frameworks; many extensions and asset swaps

Cycle stages:
  2021: euphoric boom (NVL above 90, PDR above 80, retail margin chase)
  2022: bond crisis + force-sell cascade (NVL, PDR, HPX collapsed 70–90%)
  2023: bottoming, restructuring grinds
  2024: selective recovery; VHM resilient, NVL still under pressure
  2025–26: dependent on bond rollovers and project legal unlocks

Macro sensitivity:
  Credit availability:  the #1 driver
  SBV rate moves:       direct on mortgage demand and developer cost of debt
  Bond market depth:    rollover capacity
  Government land policy: revised Land Law (effective Aug 2024) shifted procedures
```

### Steel — HPG Dominates

```
Composition:
  HPG (Hoa Phat Group) — ~40% domestic market share, integrated mill, dominant
  HSG (Hoa Sen) — galvanized steel sheets, downstream
  NKG (Nam Kim) — galvanized steel, downstream
  TVN, POM, VGS — smaller / niche

Key drivers:
  Long products (rebar / construction):
    - Domestic construction demand
    - Public infrastructure disbursement
    - Real estate launch volume
  Hot-rolled coil (HRC):
    - China HRC export price (Vietnam imports HRC for downstream conversion)
    - Vietnam-China anti-dumping disputes
  Coking coal & iron ore prices:
    - Input costs, mostly imported
  Container shipping rates:
    - Impact downstream margins

Capex cycle:
  HPG Dung Quat Phase 2: ~USD 3bn investment 2022–2025
    - Adds HRC capacity, lowers Vietnam's HRC import dependence
    - Margin expansion if completed on schedule

Investment angles:
  Public capex acceleration:  long HPG, HSG (rebar demand)
  China steel oversupply:     bearish — Chinese exports flood Vietnam at low prices
  USD strength:               bearish (input costs in USD, revenue in VND)
  Real estate recovery:       positive lag — construction demand returns ~6 months after launches
```

### Retail / Consumer Discretionary

```
Composition:
  MWG (Mobile World) — flagship; The Gioi Di Dong (mobile + ICT), Dien May Xanh (electronics),
                       Bach Hoa Xanh (BHX grocery), An Khang (pharmacy)
                       Key story: grocery rollout BHX path to profitability
  FRT (FPT Retail) — F.Studio Apple stores + Long Chau pharmacy chain
                     Long Chau is the high-growth engine, scaling to 1,500+ stores
  PNJ (Phu Nhuan Jewelry) — gold + jewelry retail, defensive cash-cow
  DGW (Digiworld) — ICT distribution

Drivers:
  Real wage growth:        primary consumption tailwind
  Consumer confidence:     fragile; tracks GSO retail sales print
  Tet seasonality:         Q1 spend surge (mobile, electronics, gold)
  Pharmacy regulation:     Long Chau benefits from pharmacy consolidation
  Mobile saturation:       MWG mobile segment maturing; growth shift to grocery + pharma

Operating model nuances:
  MWG SSSG (same-store sales growth):  watch quarterly print
  BHX store-level profitability:        critical — historic loss-maker, 2023–2024 path-to-break-even is the catalyst
  Long Chau (FRT):                      90%+ revenue growth phase, store openings 200+/yr
  Gold price (PNJ):                     gross margin sensitivity but volume effect dominates
```

### Food & Beverage / Consumer Staples

```
Composition:
  VNM (Vinamilk) — dominant dairy, defensive, dividend-yielding
  MSN (Masan Group) — diversified consumer (Masan Consumer + WinCommerce + Techcombank stake + commodity exposure)
  SAB (Sabeco) — beer, post-ThaiBev divestment, market leader
  MCH (Masan Consumer Holdings) — packaged food (instant noodles, sauces)
  KDC (Kido) — edible oil, ice cream
  PAN (PAN Group) — agribusiness conglomerate

Drivers:
  VNM:
    - Raw milk import cost (mostly imported powder, USD-priced)
    - Domestic per-capita dairy consumption rising
    - Defensive dividend (~5–6% yield)
  MSN:
    - WinCommerce (Winmart, Winmart+) profitability turn (~2024 break-even)
    - Pork prices via Masan MeatLife
    - Tungsten / chemical commodities via Masan High-Tech
    - Techcombank stake mark-to-market
  SAB:
    - Beer volume vs anti-drunk-driving enforcement (Decree 100, Dec 2019, depressed beer 30%)
    - Premium vs mainstream mix

Macro sensitivity:
  Defensive in market drawdowns
  VNM particularly defensive: positive correlation to declining VN-Index
  SAB linked to broader consumer reopening
```

### Tech / IT Services

```
Composition:
  FPT (FPT Corp) — IT services (~50% revenue), telecom, education
  CMG (CMC Corp) — IT services, smaller scale than FPT
  VGI (Viettel Global) — telecom (overseas), Viettel ecosystem
  ELC (Elcom) — telecom equipment
  SAM, DGW (peripheral)

FPT detail (the bellwether):
  Segments:
    - IT services (DX, offshore software): largest, ~50% of profit, USD-priced
    - Telecom: stable, residential broadband + data center
    - Education: FPT University, fast-growing margin
  Geographic mix: ~60% domestic, ~40% overseas (Japan + US + APAC)
  Foreign room: 100% (no FOL cap) -> chronic foreign favorite
  Valuation: 18–22x trailing PE, premium for VN large cap

Drivers:
  Global IT services demand:        Western enterprises outsourcing to Vietnam
  USD revenue mix:                   FPT benefits from USD strength
  AI / data infrastructure:          new growth segment
  Education enrollment:              defensive growth
```

### Energy / Utilities

```
Composition:
  GAS (PetroVietnam Gas) — natural gas distribution + LNG terminal
  PLX (Petrolimex) — fuel retail (regulated margin)
  POW (PetroVietnam Power) — power generation, mixed gas + hydro
  NT2 (Nhon Trach 2 Power) — gas power plant
  PVD (PV Drilling) — offshore drilling, cyclical
  PVS (PV Services) — oilfield services
  REE — diversified utilities + M&E contractor

Drivers:
  GAS:
    - Brent oil price (gas pricing linked, ~3–6 month lag)
    - LNG terminal volume (Thi Vai LNG operational from 2023)
    - Domestic gas field depletion -> LNG transition story
  PLX:
    - Regulated retail margin (MOIT adjusts every 10 days)
    - Crude price = inventory revaluation effect
    - Volume = transport activity + tourism
  POW / NT2:
    - Power generation pricing (EVN settlement)
    - Gas cost pass-through
    - Drought risk (hydro mix in POW)
  PVD / PVS:
    - Brent oil capex cycle
    - Offshore drilling day rates
```

### Aviation

```
Composition:
  VJC (VietJet Air) — private LCC, leases-heavy
  HVN (Vietnam Airlines) — SOE flag carrier, post-COVID balance-sheet stress, FSC model

Drivers:
  Tourism recovery:        China inbound critical for both
  Jet fuel:                ~30% of opex
  USD lease payments:      VJC + HVN both have USD lease cost vs VND domestic revenue
  Domestic capacity:       both airlines + Bamboo (delisted post-restructure)
  Route mix:               VJC heavier on tourist routes

VJC vs HVN:
  VJC: cleaner balance sheet, asset-light, profitable
  HVN: state-owned, debt-burdened post-COVID, recapitalization story
       Negative equity period 2021–2022, ongoing rescue
```

### Industrial Zones — China+1 Beneficiary

```
Composition:
  KBC (Kinh Bac City) — North Vietnam IZ (Bac Ninh, Hai Phong)
  SZC (Sonadezi Chau Duc) — South Vietnam (Ba Ria Vung Tau)
  IDC (Idico) — diversified IZ + energy + construction
  BCM (Becamex) — Binh Duong (HCMC adjacent)
  LHG (Long Hau) — Long An IZ
  ITA, NTC, SIP — smaller IZ developers

Business model:
  Lease industrial land to FDI manufacturers under 50-year leases
  Revenue recognized either upfront (LIRC model) or over lease term
  Cash margin: 40–60%, but land bank limited and replenishment slow

Drivers (long structural):
  China+1 manufacturing relocation (Samsung, LG, Foxconn, Intel, GoerTek, Pegatron...)
  US-China trade tension persistence
  EU CBAM and Vietnam-EU FTA
  Domestic infrastructure (port, road, power) supporting IZ utility

Drivers (cyclical / risk):
  US tariff threats on Vietnam directly (anti-circumvention)
  Land bank legal clearance (some IZs stuck)
  Sea level / climate risk in Mekong delta IZs
```

### Securities / Brokers

```
Composition:
  SSI (SSI Securities) — largest by market cap, retail + institutional
  VCI (Viet Capital) — institutional / IB focused
  HCM (HSC) — institutional, listed for many years
  VND (VNDirect) — retail, fintech-style
  VIX, BSI, SHS, MBS, FTS, AGR — smaller / mid

Drivers:
  Market trading volume:      revenue from brokerage commission
  Margin balance:             interest income on margin lending (now ~40–60% of profit for many)
  Proprietary book P&L:       can swing earnings quarter to quarter
  Investment banking fees:    M&A advisory, equity / bond underwriting
  CW issuance:                fee + market-making P&L for licensed CW issuers (SSI, VPS, HSC, MBS, VCSC)

High-beta plays on the broader market — when VN-Index rallies, brokers rally 2x typically.
```

## Sector Rotation Patterns

### Macro Trigger -> Sector Response

```
SBV rate cut announcement:
  Day 1-5:   Banks rally (NIM compression worry initially) -> often dip then recover
  Day 1-5:   Real estate rally (mortgage cost relief)
  Day 1-5:   Securities rally (volume tailwind)
  Day 5-30:  Real estate continues; banks consolidate

Credit growth quota top-up (mid-year):
  Banks rally (loan book growth)
  Followed by real estate developers (financing availability)

Public capex acceleration:
  Steel (HPG, HSG) -> Cement (HT1, BCC) -> Construction (VCG, HHV, C4G)
  Stone (KSB, DHA) -> Industrial zones (KBC, SZC) downstream

VND depreciation > 3%:
  Beneficiaries:    FPT (USD revenue), exporters (textiles unlisted)
  Casualties:       Aviation (HVN > VJC), pharma, steel (input cost)
  Mixed:            Banks (some USD exposure but VND assets dominant)

China steel price collapse:
  Casualties:       HPG (Vietnam vs imported steel margin pressure)
  Beneficiaries:    Construction contractors (cheaper input)

Tet (Lunar New Year) approaching:
  Retail spend surge: MWG (mobile), PNJ (gold), F&B
  Aviation peak: VJC domestic routes
  Then post-Tet drag for retail in March

Earnings season:
  Q4 results (Jan–Feb):    Banks tend to lead post-Tet
  Q1 results (Apr–May):    Real estate weak (low pre-sale recognition), tech / consumer staples strong
  Q2 results (Jul–Aug):    VN30 rebalance + earnings -> sector tilts
  Q3 results (Oct–Nov):    Q4 capex push + earnings -> steel / construction tilt
```

### Calendar Anchors for Rotation

```
January:   Tet retail spend (MWG, PNJ); SBV credit quota announcement (banks)
February:  Tet break + post-Tet Q4 earnings (banks lead)
March:     Q1 GDP preview, AGM season starts (real estate dividend cuts watched)
April:     AGM peak (April 30 + May 1 holidays around it)
May-June:  Public capex picks up (steel + construction); semiannual VN30 rebalance late Jun
July:      VN30 rebalance effective; Q2 earnings begin
August:    Q2 earnings season peak; banks NPL print scrutiny
September: Q3 starts; September 2 holiday; back-to-school for FPT Education
October:   Q3 earnings begin; National Assembly session (next year targets)
November:  Q3 earnings + capex sprint (steel rally common)
December:  Year-end window-dressing; final capex disbursement; bond maturity wall watch
```

## Notes

1. **Sector definitions vary**: GICS and Vietnamese broker classifications differ; Vingroup is sometimes classified as conglomerate (VIC) but split as real estate (VHM), retail (VRE), auto (VFS-US listed)
2. **Vingroup ecosystem concentration risk**: VIC + VHM + VRE + VEF + parent debt = single ecosystem with cross-exposures; sizing should treat as one cluster
3. **State ownership skew**: many sectors have SOE-private divergence (banks, energy, aviation) — execution quality differs
4. **Foreign room caps cluster by sector**: banks (30%) hit room frequently; non-bank large caps (49%) less commonly maxed out
5. **Single-stock concentration**: VCB alone is ~10% of VN-Index, HPG dominates steel, MWG dominates retail discretionary — beta to single stock can be high
6. **Earnings revision direction matters more than absolute level**: VN analyst coverage thinner than developed markets; consensus revisions move stocks more than the print itself in some sectors
7. **Use vn-equity-market skill for FOL / liquidity context, vn-macro for top-down view, vn-derivatives for hedging mechanics**
